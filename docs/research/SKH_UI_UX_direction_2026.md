# Make SKH an AI-first, evidence-led green workbench

Semantic Knowledge Hub should be **AI-first**: the first thing on every screen is one field to ask or search, and AI assistance sits on every object (document, proposal, search result, graph node, task). What makes this safe for a bank, and what sets SKH apart, is that the AI is **evidence-led**. Answers lead with quoted passages and server-verified citations. "Insufficient evidence" is a structured result with next steps. Every AI change goes through one propose → diff → review → GitLab MR pipeline, so nothing the AI writes becomes knowledge without a person approving it. Neither Plane nor Linear documents source citations in AI answers, so this evidence layer is SKH's real differentiator, not a gap to fill by copying. Around the AI sits a **familiar chassis from Plane and Linear**: a light, collapsible left sidebar, a slim top bar that holds the ask-or-search composer (⌘K), the context panel (⌘J) and the AI-mode chip, one standard header and view bar on every screen, Peek side-sheets, and one collection view for lists, tables, boards and timelines. The **finish is Apple-grade but opaque-first**: one prominent action per toolbar, purposeful motion, semantic tokens, and no glass in the content layer. AI-first is designed for all four AI modes. SKH's default deployments run `mock` or `disabled`, so every AI surface has a manual path and the app stays complete with AI off. The phase‑1 "ledger green" redesign fixed identity and home-page hierarchy, but it left a platform built for five pages: no component primitives, no icons, no dark mode, no shell-level search or composer, no overlays and no full-bleed layouts. Its near-black bar, full-width yellow demo band and "approved = brand green" rule also clash with the Plane-like calm the owner wants. Green stays the brand and becomes the single interactive accent; approval gets a quiet, icon-led mark, because in a knowledge base "approved" is the normal state. The roadmap leads with the AI-first entry points (composer, evidence-first answers, AI-mode chip), which can ship on today's components, then builds the platform (tokens v2, primitives, shell v2, themes) that AI on every object and the later graph, kanban and Gantt views depend on. Those modules stay views over real adapters that show "не подключено" until connected.

## The 2026 state of the art: structure endures, skins churn

The clearest signal from 2025–26 is that **every leading product rebuilt its visual skin but kept its interaction primitives**. Raycast shipped a ground-up 2.0 with its own indexer and then re-skinned all UI again for a new macOS look within four months ([Raycast blog](https://raycast.com/blog/the-new-raycast), [Raycast changelog](https://www.raycast.com/changelog)). Linear redesigned in March 2024 and again in March 2026 and kept Peek, J/K and its command grammar intact ([Linear 2024](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui), [Linear 2026](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). Plane replaced three component libraries with one system, Propel, and stated that "screen layouts are unchanged" ([Plane, Propel](https://plane.so/blog/propel-plane-design-system)). The command palette, collapsible sidebar, slash menu, structured properties over Markdown and reviewable AI output are the durable layer. Translucent skins and novelty interactions are the fashion layer.

**Calm is now an explicit design goal, achieved by subtraction.** Linear's 2026 refresh is titled "a calmer interface". It dimmed the sidebar "a few notches", shrank icons, removed coloured team-icon backgrounds, cut dividers ("structure should be felt not seen") and warmed its greys ([Linear, Behind the latest design refresh, 12 Mar 2026](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). Plane's Navigation 2.0 promised navigation that feels "clear, calm, and in your control". It moved global tools such as Search, Inbox and AI out of the project tree into a fixed top bar ([Plane Navigation 2.0, 2 Dec 2025](https://plane.so/blog/introducing-plane-navigation-2)). Atlassian's new navigation likewise decluttered the top bar to "search and create" around a resizable, collapsible sidebar ([Atlassian, 16 Mar 2025](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/introducing-new-navigation)). Vercel moved its tabs into a hideable sidebar in February 2026 ([Vercel changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/dashboard-navigation-redesign-rollout)). The converged shell is an **inverted L**: a sidebar for the content hierarchy and a thin top bar for global tools. The chrome sits deliberately lower in contrast than the content.

**Colour systems are generated, perceptual and role-mapped.** Linear replaced 98 hand-set theme variables with three LCH inputs (base, accent, contrast), and the contrast input yields high-contrast themes automatically ([Linear 2024](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Plane's Propel tokens use OKLCH neutrals with near-zero chroma and a canvas → surface → layer elevation model. Hover, active and selected variants are built in, and the system ships Light, Dark, Light high-contrast and Dark high-contrast themes plus forced-colors and reduced-motion stylesheets ([npm @makeplane/propel 0.9.10](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@makeplane/propel), [Plane AGENTS.md](https://github.com/makeplane/plane/blob/preview/packages/tailwind-config/AGENTS.md)). Radix, Geist and Primer all define each step of a hue scale by its UI role (app background, component hover, border, solid fill, text), not by lightness alone ([Radix Colors](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale), [Geist Colors](https://vercel.com/geist/colors), [Primer colour usage](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/color-usage/)). Primer's high-contrast themes target **≥7:1** ([Primer](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/color-usage/)). Atlassian treats density (cozy/comfortable/compact), reduced motion and typography as token themes alongside colour ([Atlassian tokens](https://atlassian.design/foundations/tokens/design-tokens)).

**Apple's 2025–26 guidance is more useful for its discipline than its glass.** The HIG now splits the UI into a floating functional layer (Liquid Glass, for controls and navigation) and a content layer. It is explicit: "**Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer**" and "use Liquid Glass effects sparingly" ([HIG Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). NN/g found that "text on top of text creates an illegible mess" and that the interface "is shouting 'look at me' when it should quietly step aside" ([NN/g, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Apple then walked the look back in stages. It added a Clear/Tinted option in 26.1 ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2025/11/soaping-up-liquid-glass-less-transparency-more-contrast/)). In 2026 it added a clear-to-tinted slider, darkened edges and the return of a uniform toolbar ([MacRumors, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27/)). On the web, `prefers-reduced-transparency` is still experimental and not Baseline ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-transparency)). Chris Coyier warns that the look "has been rightfully criticized for text contrast accessibility" ([Frontend Masters blog](https://blog.master.dev/liquid-glass-on-the-web/)). What does transfer from Apple: at most two sidebar levels, with a list–detail split for deeper trees; at most three toolbar groups; one prominent primary action on the trailing side; every toolbar action also available as a command; one colour never meaning two things; and light, dark and increased-contrast variants for every colour ([HIG Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars), [HIG Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars), [HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)).

**AI has moved from a destination to an attribute of objects.** Notion's agent scopes to the current page or selection by default ([Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/en-gb/help/notion-agent)). Linear's agent opens with Cmd/Ctrl+J. Its Triage Intelligence puts suggestions on the property they would change, with accept, decline and "view why" ([Linear Agent](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-24-introducing-linear-agent), [Triage Intelligence](https://linear.app/docs/triage-intelligence)). Plane AI splits Ask (read-only), Build ("Nothing changes until you click Confirm") and Autopilot ([Plane AI docs](https://docs.plane.so/ai/pi-chat)). Expert users push back when changes land before review. Cursor users called session-level-only review "show me damage after the fact" ([Cursor forum, May 2026](https://forum.cursor.com/t/bring-back-per-change-apply-inline-diff-review-you-re-throwing-away-your-best-ux-advantage/160856)). Research adds an important caution. **Citations raise trust even when they are random**, and trust drops once users check them ([Ding et al., AAAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01303v1)). Explanations increase reliance on both right and wrong answers, while real sources reduce reliance on wrong ones ([Kim et al. 2025, N=308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08554v1)). Colour-coded uncertainty highlighting improved error detection ([Spatharioti et al., CHI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1134789)). A citation marker is therefore not a safeguard unless the server verifies it.

## Plane and Linear set the bar; eight others fill specific gaps

Linear's advantage is **coherence through restraint**. Its model is deliberately narrow: teams own workflows, there is one issue type and few configurable fields. Plane's own comparison concedes "If you want a fast and focused issue tracker… Linear is exactly that" ([Plane vs Linear](https://plane.so/blog/plane-versus-linear-which-should-you-choose-in-2026)). Fewer entity types mean fewer UI states, and Linear's 2026 refresh standardised headers and view controls across projects, issues, reviews and documents ([Linear changelog 2026-03-12](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-12-ui-refresh)). Its weakness for SKH is that docs are subordinate to work, and its exact tokens are unpublished. One third-party measurement puts Linear's faintest text at about 3.6:1, below AA, but that figure is unverified ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/linear-aesthetic-tokens-density-keyboard-first-ux-2026)).

Plane's advantage is **breadth that matches SKH's roadmap, plus inspectable rigour**. It has List, Board, Calendar, Table and a Timeline with dependencies and critical path. Its workspace Wiki has collections, version history and synced blocks. It runs self-hosted and air-gapped, ships a Russian locale, and its tokens are open-source ([Plane layouts](https://docs.plane.so/core-concepts/issues/layouts), [Plane Pages](https://docs.plane.so/core-concepts/pages/overview), [Plane i18n](https://github.com/makeplane/plane/tree/preview/packages/i18n/src/locales)). Its weakness is self-admitted debt. Before Propel it ran three component libraries, and "small differences built up between screens" ([Propel](https://plane.so/blog/propel-plane-design-system)). Before Navigation 2.0 its sidebar was long and cluttered ([Nav 2.0](https://plane.so/blog/introducing-plane-navigation-2)). G2 reviewers still call it "unpolished" and say "AI was added without being integrated well" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/plane-2024-10-30/reviews), small sample). Plane is AGPL‑3.0, so SKH should borrow **patterns and token ideas, not code**, unless legal review clears it.

## Phase 1 fixed identity but left SKH without a platform

The phase‑1 redesign is a real improvement over the original UI. The empty navy sidebar became a green top bar, and content width grew from about 1,130 to 1,248 px. The home page now has one filled "Найти" instead of three equal hero buttons. List titles are ink instead of underlined links. The lifecycle track works horizontally on desktop and vertically on phones **[verified in app]**. The typeface trio of Golos Text for UI, PT Serif for prose and JetBrains Mono for IDs reads calm and editorial in Cyrillic **[verified in app]**. The feedback semantics are better than most enterprise apps: HTTP-aware Russian error titles, a request ID, a "Конфликт версий" state, live regions and section-level partial failure **[inferred from code]**. Phase 1 should be treated as a solid identity layer that now needs a platform underneath it.

The core problem is that **the "design system" is 334 CSS selectors with no primitives layer** **[inferred from code]**. There are about 16 React components, mostly domain widgets, and no Button, Field, Tabs, Dialog, Menu, Popover, Tooltip or Icon. Not a single icon appears in any screenshot **[verified in app]**. The consequences are visible. Seven different badge-like markers appear, and one home-page proposal row carries two bordered badges plus reason plus timestamp **[verified in app]**. A dashed border means "draft" for documents and "Новое" for proposals. Blue means both "in review" and plain information notices **[verified in app]**. Selected segments use a dark green fill that is nearly identical to the primary button: "Команда" next to "Спросить" on Ask, and the "Все" filter on Proposals **[verified in app]**. This breaks phase 1's own "one filled green per view" rule. There are 14 hard-coded hex values, including `#fff` fifteen times, and 13 font sizes outside the scale **[inferred from code]**. That blocks dark mode until it is cleaned up.

The palette is dignified but heavy. A near-black `#143826` bar sits above a full-width cream-yellow demo band on every page, so every screen opens with two strong horizontal slabs **[verified in app]**. The band wraps to four lines on phones and pushes the H1 to about 180 px **[verified in app]**. The demo message is repeated three times on a document page **[verified in app]**. "Approved" `#23683f` is deliberately the brand green and is visually close to the primary button `#1d5c3c` **[inferred from code]**. That conflicts with Apple's rule against one colour carrying interactive and non-interactive meaning ([HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)) **[industry research]**. Small text dominates: 13 px is used 53 times against 9 for the 15 px body **[inferred from code]**. Four rules ask for weight 700, which Golos does not import, so the browser synthesises a faux bold **[inferred from code]**.

The proposal page shows the workflow problems most clearly. Three full-width notices sit between the lifecycle track and the content. The five-button "Действия" row (continue, ready for review, send to GitLab (disabled), reject, delete) is about 1,780 px down, below the full diff **[verified in app]**. The propose-change screen exposes raw Markdown **with YAML front-matter** in a monospace textarea. That contradicts the new-document wizard's promise that "Разбираться в Git и YAML не нужно" **[verified in app]**. Dates use two formats on the same screen and wrap onto three lines in the proposals table **[verified in app]**. On a 390 px phone the proposals table is cut off and scrolls sideways, and its nine filter chips wrap to four rows **[verified in app]**.

The structure is not ready for the planned modules **[inferred from code]**. A flat five-link bar cannot hold the roughly ten destinations that graph, tasks, board, Gantt and planning would add. Every route is capped at 78 rem, but boards, timelines and graphs need full-bleed layouts. There are no overlays, drag handles, virtualization, icons, elevation, z-index or motion tokens. ## Three directions differ in what they put at the centre

The three directions are not accent swaps. Each puts something different at the centre: **A** centres the document and craft, **B** centres the collection and throughput, and **C** centres the question and its evidence.

### Direction A — "Quiet instrument" (Apple-inspired productivity)

**Philosophy:** Apple's layered model applied to the web. A thin functional layer recedes, and the content layer (documents, diffs) carries all the contrast, following the rule that controls should "quietly step aside" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). **Visual character:** generous whitespace and a light, high-opacity toolbar with a scroll-edge separation instead of a filled bar ([HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)). Radii are concentric. There are almost no borders and no shadows except on overlays. **Layout:** a macOS-style three-pane split view (sidebar, content list, detail), plus an inspector that opens from the trailing toolbar. Sidebars hold at most two levels ([HIG Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)). **Navigation:** a sidebar with sources and collections, and a toolbar with at most three groups and one prominent action. **Typography:** a large, airy reading measure (16/26 prose). The UI body is 14 px, and the title hierarchy is expressed by weight more than size. **Colour:** near-white canvas, greys and a single green accent. Users can pick small/medium/large sidebar rows, as macOS allows. **Components:** segmented controls drawn as raised segments on a sunk track, popovers, sheets and inspectors. **Interactions:** short, cancellable motion and full menu/command parity. Contextual menus carry most actions. **Benefits:** the best reading and review experience, the highest perceived craft, and the strongest fit with "Apple-quality". **Drawbacks:** low information density for kanban, Gantt and tables. A three-pane split struggles with Russian label lengths at 1280 px. Teams also tend to copy the skin (glass, faux window chrome) rather than the discipline. Without strong rules, A drifts into the superficial macOS imitation the brief warns against. **Suitability:** excellent for Knowledge Base, Document and Review; weak for Work modules.

### Direction B — "Green workbench" (modern enterprise SaaS, Plane/Linear)

**Philosophy:** speed and consistency through one standard frame. Every screen is a location bar, a view bar and a collection, and Linear's rule is "don't compete for attention you haven't earned" ([Linear 2026](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)). **Visual character:** low-chroma, slightly green-tinted neutrals. Hairline borders, compact pills and dense rows (32 px) appear in Compact mode. Colour is reserved for state, priority and source. **Layout:** an inverted L. Collections are full-width, and object detail opens in a right Peek sheet. Pages use three width modes (prose, standard, full-bleed). **Navigation:** a slim sidebar (Knowledge / Work / System, pinned items, icon-only collapse) and a top bar holding ⌘K, Inbox/Reviews, the AI toggle and identity. Everything is reachable through a G/O/N-style command grammar ([Plane Power K](https://plane.so/blog/introducing-power-k-keyboard-first-navigation-and-actions)). **Typography:** 13/14 px UI with tabular numerals, using the Label vs Copy split from Geist. **Colour:** a Propel-like canvas/surface/layer model in OKLCH with one green accent. **Components:** a data table, filter chips with `@` syntax, a display-options popover, a property pill row, a Peek sheet and toasts with undo. **Interactions:** keyboard-first (J/K, Space, X select, bulk edit) with inline property editing. **Benefits:** scales across every planned module, is the most familiar to the owner (who likes Plane), is well documented in open-source tokens, and is quickest to make consistent. **Drawbacks:** it can feel generic ("another Linear clone"). Long-form reading and AI evidence are second-class. Taken as-is, it imports Plane's failure mode of navigation breadth. **Suitability:** excellent for Search, Proposals, Tasks, Board, Timeline and Integrations; adequate for documents.

### Direction C — "Evidence canvas" (AI-native knowledge workspace)

**Philosophy:** the question is the primary unit of work. The home is an "ask or search" composer, and every screen is a working set of sources that the AI and the user share, extending Dia's "tabs are our core context" ([Browser Company](https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/letter-to-arc-members-2025)). **Visual character:** a persistent right context rail with source chips. Answers are laid out as cards of quoted evidence, and the local graph is a first-class panel. **Layout:** a three-zone workspace with the sources/working set on the left, the document or canvas in the centre, and the AI and evidence rail on the right. Spatial or graph views replace some lists. **Navigation:** composer-first. Collections are reached through the AI, the graph or the palette rather than a tree. **Typography:** prose-forward, with mono for identifiers and quotes styled as evidence. **Colour:** neutral, with **source hues** (gitlab / local / confluence) as the dominant colour signal, plus categorical uncertainty highlighting. **Components:** answer card, citation hover-card, support-level marks per claim, agent session panel (plan, activity, stop), context chips and an "insufficient evidence" card. **Interactions:** select text to ask, propose a change from an answer, assign a task to an AI delegate, and save answers as notes with live citations. **Benefits:** the strongest differentiation and the best fit for grounded answers. It puts SKH's unique provenance rules on the surface. **Drawbacks:** it makes AI the default in a deployment where `mock`/disabled is the default. It risks Arc's "novelty tax". A graph-led layout runs into the documented low value of global graphs as navigation ([Konik essay](https://www.eleanorkonik.com/its-not-just-a-pretty-gimmick-in-defense-of-obsidians-graph-view); Obsidian users' filtering requests). It is the hardest to make accessible, and it erodes the "AI is optional" guidance ([HIG Generative AI](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)). **Suitability:** excellent for Ask, Document context and Review evidence; poor as the whole shell.

| Attribute | A Quiet instrument | B Green workbench | C Evidence canvas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Centre of gravity | Document | Collection | Question + evidence |
| Shell | Split view + inspector | Inverted L + Peek | Three-zone workspace |
| Default density | Comfortable | Compact for work, comfortable for reading | Comfortable |
| Chrome contrast | Lowest | Low | Medium (rails always visible) |
| Colour's job | Accent only | State, priority, source | Source and certainty |
| AI placement | Inspector tab | Cmd+J panel and inline suggestions | Always-on rail and composer home |
| Biggest risk | Skin imitation, low density | Generic feel | Novelty tax, AI-off mode feels broken |

## Recommendation: an AI-first hybrid — C's centre, B's chassis, A's finish

The owner asked for SKH to be AI-first. That makes Direction C's centre of gravity (the question and its evidence) the centre of the product. The research still argues against taking C whole: its composer-only navigation, always-on rail and graph-led layout carry Arc's "novelty tax", score worst on accessibility, and break when AI is off. So the recommendation keeps B's chassis and A's finishing rules and moves C's question-first interaction into that frame. The matrix below scores each direction from 1 (poor) to 5 (strong) for SKH specifically. Scores are my judgement against the evidence above. Implementation complexity is scored so that 5 means simplest. The "layered hybrid" column is the earlier recommendation (AI as a layer on B's shell), kept for comparison.

| Criterion | A | B | C | Layered hybrid (earlier) | **AI-first hybrid (recommended)** | Main reason for the AI-first score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Visual quality | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | **5** | A's discipline applied to B's frame; AI uses the same tokens, no AI styling |
| Usability | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **4** | Ask-first entry is fast for newcomers, but less familiar than a plain catalogue; search fallback keeps experts fast |
| Information density | 2 | 5 | 3 | 4 | **4** | Collections and density modes from B are unchanged |
| Accessibility | 4 | 4 | 2 | 4 | **4** | No always-on rail or canvas navigation; the composer is a standard combobox |
| Enterprise credibility | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **4** | AI as the front door needs visible guardrails (mode chip, labels, review) to read as trustworthy in a bank |
| AI-native compatibility | 3 | 3 | 5 | 5 | **5** | AI on every object and as the entry point |
| Scalability across modules | 2 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **5** | Same `CollectionView`; the context panel mounts on any object |

| **Total (of 50)** | **33** | **44** | **31** | **44** | **42** | |

The unweighted total does not by itself favour AI-first: the earlier layered hybrid scores higher on usability, credibility and complexity (its fit score drops from 5 to 4 here because the vision now includes AI-first). Choosing AI-first is a product-strategy decision, and the costs are known: more implementation work, more states to test, and a front door that must earn trust. The guardrails below are what keep those costs bounded.

**Recommend the AI-first hybrid, provisionally named "Green workbench, AI-first".** B is still the chassis, because it is the only direction that scales to search, proposals, tasks, board, timeline and integrations on one component set. That scaling is what lets Linear feel consistent and what Plane had to rebuild Propel to get. A still supplies the finishing rules: opaque-first surfaces, one prominent action per toolbar, at most three toolbar groups, purposeful motion, and a comfortable reading mode. From C, the AI-first version takes more than the layered version did:

- **Ask-or-search composer as the entry point.** One field in the top bar (⌘K, `/`) and as the home hero: questions, search, navigation and commands. Typing shows instant matches; Enter on a question asks the AI; Tab switches between "Спросить ИИ" and "Найти". This is Raycast's pattern (type to search, Tab to ask AI) ([Raycast 2.0](https://www.raycast.com/changelog/macos/2-0)) and Plane's top-bar placement of AI next to search ([Plane Navigation 2.0](https://plane.so/blog/introducing-plane-navigation-2)).
- **The answer as a first-class result view**, evidence first, with a structured insufficient-evidence card.
- **A context panel on every object page** (⌘J, as in Linear's agent ([Linear Agent](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-24-introducing-linear-agent))), scoped to the object and to a selection, with the actions "Объяснить", "Найти связанные", "Проверить актуальность", "Предложить правку". Threads attach to the object, not to a global chat history.
- **AI in every module**: an answer card above question-like catalogue queries, natural language turned into visible, editable filter chips, an AI review summary on proposals, path explanations on the knowledge map, and AI delegates on tasks.

What still stays out of C: an always-open AI rail (the panel is collapsible and remembers its state), graph-led navigation (the knowledge map is a secondary view), composer-only navigation (the sidebar and catalogue remain), and any feature that exists only behind a prompt. This follows the Dia lesson: a familiar shell, with the novelty in what the shell can do ([Browser Company](https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/letter-to-arc-members-2025)).

**AI-first has to work in all four AI modes**, because `mock`/`disabled` is SKH's default ([HIG Generative AI](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai) also asks for a non-AI fallback):

| Surface | `internal_api` / `claude_cli` | `mock` | `disabled` |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI-mode chip | "ИИ: внутренняя модель" / "ИИ: Claude CLI (эксперимент)" | "ИИ: демо", striped | "ИИ отключён" |
| Composer | Asks AI; search results listed underneath | Answers with verbatim excerpts only, labelled "Демо" | Search only; placeholder "Найти в базе знаний…" |
| Context panel | All actions | Generation actions shown disabled with a one-line reason | Hidden; ⌘J opens search |
| AI drafts | Available | Template + verbatim excerpts, labelled | Manual editor only, no dead buttons |

The adopt and avoid decisions follow. **Adopt** these: the ask-or-search composer as the top-bar and home entry point, with a visible trigger as well as ⌘K. The context panel (⌘J) on every object page. The AI-mode chip with a popover naming provider, model, retrieval scope and limits. The inverted-L shell, with a light sidebar grouped Знания / Работа / Система, pinned items and icon-only collapse (Plane Nav 2.0, Atlassian). One location bar and one view bar on every screen (Linear 2026). Peek sheets that open on Space *and* on click, since Linear's keyboard-only Peek would be invisible to mouse users ([Peek](https://linear.app/docs/peek)). One `CollectionView` with saved views that separate filters from display options. The canvas/surface/layer elevation model. Comfortable and Compact density. Light, dark and both high-contrast themes, plus forced-colors support. Plane's Build-style confirm cards and Linear's highlighted agent edits, author indicators and version checkpoints. Carbon's AI label, which turns into a revert control once a human edits the text ([Carbon AI label](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/ai-label/usage/)). GitHub's rule that the requester of an AI change cannot be its only approver ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/use-copilot-agents/cloud-agent/review-copilot-prs)).

**Deliberately avoid** the following. No glass or blur in the content layer, and no faux macOS window chrome. No "AI sparkle" icons, gradients, glowing borders or decorative typing animations: AI uses the same tokens as everything else plus a neutral AI label. No second chat box: the composer and one context panel serve every page. No proactive AI nudges by default; the user opens AI. No numeric confidence percentages, since PAIR calls them risky ([PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)); use categorical support levels instead. No Autopilot mode for anything that touches knowledge. No auto-applied AI edits. No AI-only features. No icon-only tab pills, because Russian users cannot rely on learned icons for unfamiliar actions. No permanent sidebar slot for the global graph. No near-black bar slab. No full-width demo band; use a striped "Демо" pill in the top bar plus labels on demo output. Do not mix team (GitLab) and corporate (Confluence) results in one ungrouped list.

### Resolving the green-brand versus "approved" conflict

Today the conflict is structural. Brand green fills the bar, primary buttons, links and selected toggles, *and* marks approved documents. Apple warns against exactly this ([HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). The research offers three options: a restrained brand green with success on a different hue, a non-green interactive accent, or green accent with approval shown in neutral plus an icon. Since the owner prefers green, option (b) is off the table. The recommendation combines (a) and (c) and rests on a domain insight. **In a curated knowledge base, approved is the baseline state, and the exceptions are what need signal.** So brand green does exactly one job: it is the interactive accent for primary actions, links, focus rings and the active nav item. It sits on near-neutral, faintly green-tinted chrome. "Approved" becomes a quiet, icon-led mark: a check-seal glyph plus the word "Утверждён" in ink or a muted success tone, never a solid green fill. Draft (amber, dashed), in review (a dedicated review hue, *not* the info blue), stale (amber with a clock), deprecated (dark grey, struck through, with a successor banner) and rejected (red) carry the louder treatments. The `success` token used for "approved", "connected" and "passed" should sit at least about 20° of OKLCH hue away from the brand, for example towards teal, and appear only in muted variants. For illustration, a teal `#0f6b6b` on its wash `#e3f1ef` computes to 5.42:1 and on the current paper to 5.67:1 (my computation; values to be validated). Selected states use a raised neutral segment or an accent-wash with an accent-ink border, never a solid accent fill. These rules restore "one filled green per view". On the accent itself: the current `#1d5c3c` gives 7.93:1 under white text but reads almost black. A mid-luminance green near `#18693f` (6.71:1 with white) or `#1f7a4d` (5.32:1) is livelier and still passes AA for button text (my computations). The dark-mode accent must be a separately tuned lighter step, not an inversion. For example, `#4cc38a` on a `#141615` surface computes to 8.21:1, and it should carry dark text on solid fills (8.71:1).

## Design-system blueprint: tokens first, primitives second

The blueprint is conceptual, not code. It borrows Primer's base → functional → component token tiers, Atlassian's `foundation.property.modifier` naming and the role-mapped steps of Geist and Radix ([Primer](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/color-usage/), [Atlassian tokens](https://atlassian.design/foundations/tokens/design-tokens), [Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/colors)). The token source should be a JSON file that can sync to Figma, which is what Linear's 2026 team did ([Linear 2026](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh)).

| Area | Blueprint |
|---|---|
| **Typography** | Keep **Golos Text** for UI, as it is already bundled self-hosted, offline-safe and reviewed as "decent" Cyrillic ([type.today](https://type.today/en/journal/neo)). Weights 400/500/600 only; remove the 700 rules or import 700. Keep **JetBrains Mono** for IDs, paths, SQL and YAML. Keep **PT Serif** only for long-form reading in the viewer; use sans in the editor and diffs. Evaluate **IBM Plex Sans** as the alternative if tables dominate: it has the best-reviewed Cyrillic and tabular digits by default ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource/ibm-plex-sans)). Inter only after checking that its 4.1 Cyrillic overhaul answers the critique ([rsms/inter](https://github.com/rsms/inter/releases)). Scale: 12/16 caption (minimum, tertiary meta only), 13/18 dense table, 14/20 UI body, 16/24 reading body (prose 16/26), 18/24, 20/28, 24/32 page title. Separate Label (single-line) and Copy (multi-line) styles. `tabular-nums` for tables, dates, counters and diff gutters. Reading measure 60–75 characters. |

Then the AI-mode chip, Ревью (count includes AI drafts awaiting the user), demo pill and identity; ⌘J toggles the object's context panel. Sidebar with at most two levels (Apple) and a list–detail split for deeper GitLab trees. Groups: Знания (Документы by collection, Карта знаний as a secondary view), Работа (Предложения/Ревью, Задачи when connected), Система (Интеграции, Настройки). Pinned items, recently viewed and recent answers. Breadcrumb location bar and view bar as one component. |

| **Interaction** | Keyboard grammar: ⌘K, `/` search, G→ go, O→ open, N→ new, J/K move, Space peek, X select, `?` shortcut sheet. Click opens Peek and ⌘/Ctrl-click opens the full page. | **Motion** | Durations 120–250 ms, ease-out, used only for spatial continuity (sheet in/out, toast, reorder). | **Accessibility** | WCAG 2.2 AA baseline, targeting 2.4.13 focus appearance (2 px ring, ≥3:1 in both states). `scroll-padding` equals the sticky header height (2.4.11). Honour `prefers-color-scheme`, `prefers-contrast: more`, `forced-colors: active` and `prefers-reduced-motion`, with in-app overrides because OS signals are unreliable on the web. |
| **Responsive** | Desktop-first. Below lg the sidebar collapses to icons and the AI panel becomes an overlay sheet. Below md, tables become stacked list rows and filters sit behind "Фильтры (n)". Functionality stays the same and only its visibility changes ([HIG Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)). Russian-length budget: short labels can run 180–300% of English at ≤20 characters ([W3C i18n](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size)), so labels truncate with tooltips, toolbars overflow into "Ещё", and tabs never have fixed widths. `hyphens: auto` with `lang="ru"` in narrow columns. |

The AI layer deserves its own specification, because this is where SKH can lead. **Answers lead with quoted evidence, not fluent rationale**, because explanations raise overreliance while sources reduce it ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08554v1)). Each claim carries an inline numbered marker. Hovering shows a card with title, source badge, version or commit SHA and the quoted passage. Clicking opens the document in Peek, scrolled to the highlighted passage. This combines the Glean and NotebookLM patterns. The server must check that every cited span exists in the retrieved chunk; claims that fail the check are dropped or marked "нет подтверждения". Support levels are categorical: "подтверждено цитатой / частично / нет подтверждения". The **insufficient-evidence state is a structured card**, not an apology. It shows what was searched and in which scope, the closest partial matches with citations, and next actions: refine the question, run a *separate, labelled* corporate Confluence query, or create a draft or task to document the gap. This applies HAX G10 "scope services when in doubt" ([HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/)). **The AI-mode chip** ("ИИ: демо (mock)" / "ИИ: внутренняя модель" / "ИИ отключён") opens a popover with the provider, model, retrieval scope and limits, using Carbon's Overview / Supporting details / Artifacts / Actions template. Demo output gets a striped "Демо" label so a screenshot cannot pass for a real answer. Team and corporate scopes stay as separate retrieval contexts, with badges on every citation. Conversation threads attach to an object (document, draft, task), not to a global history. **Agents are delegates**: the human stays the owner, the agent has a visible plan, an activity log (thought / action / elicitation / response / error, from Linear's taxonomy ([Linear developers](https://linear.app/developers/agent-interaction))) and a Stop button, and its output is always a draft or MR.

**AI-first copy (Russian UI).** Composer: "Спросить или найти…" (AI off: "Найти в базе знаний…"). Support levels: "Подтверждено источниками" / "Частично подтверждено" / "Недостаточно данных"; unverified claim: "Нет подтверждения в источниках". Insufficient-evidence title: "В базе знаний нет ответа на этот вопрос". AI label: "ИИ"; after a human edit: "Вернуть вариант ИИ". Draft origin: "Черновик подготовлен ИИ — нужна проверка". Requester rule: "Вы запросили этот черновик, поэтому утвердить его должен другой участник". Streaming stages: "Ищу источники → Формулирую ответ → Проверяю цитаты". **AI tokens:** `ai.label` (neutral ink on a faint tint), `ai.unsupported` (amber underline for claims without support) and `source.{gitlab,local,confluence,mock}` at equal lightness. **The test for every screen:** a new analyst reaches the right approved document by asking, and a reviewer verifies any AI claim in two clicks (citation → highlighted passage).

## Roadmap: AI-first entry, then the platform, then AI on every object

No timelines are given, because there is no technical basis for estimating them. Order matters more than dates. AI-first changes the order of the earlier roadmap: the AI entry points come first, because they can ship on today's components and deliver the AI-first experience early. The platform still has to come before AI on every object and before the graph, kanban and Gantt modules, otherwise those would be rebuilt when primitives arrive. Every AI item must pass in `mock` and `disabled` mode, not only with a real model.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "AI-first" should mean for SKH. It should not mean a chat window in place of the product. It should mean that asking is the fastest way in, that AI is available on every object, and that every AI answer is something a reviewer can check in two clicks. That is where both Plane and Linear are silent and where SKH can lead: verified, passage-level citations, explicit separation of team and corporate sources, an honest refusal state, and AI drafts that go through the same review as human edits. The trust research makes this more than polish. Unverified citations make users *more* confident in wrong answers, so an AI-first product without verification would be worse than no AI at all.

"Apple-quality" is likewise a set of disciplines rather than a style: subtraction, one meaning per colour, one primary action, layers that never compete with content, and respect for user settings. Apple itself spent 2026 walking its glass back toward opacity and contrast. "Plane-like" is less about Plane's pixels than about its open token architecture and its shell split between global tools and the content hierarchy. AI-first fits that shell well, because Plane already puts AI next to search in the top bar.

The most consequential decision is **order**. The AI-first entry points (composer, evidence-first answers, mode chip, insufficient-evidence card) can ship on today's components and deliver the AI-first experience early. AI on every object, and every later module, should wait for the platform (tokens v2, primitives, shell v2, themes), otherwise SKH would recreate the three-library problem Plane spent 2026 fixing. The main uncertainties to close before implementation: the live app was not observed (no dark, hover, loading or 1024 px states); Linear's actual tokens and both competitors' hands-on feel were not inspected; the green and teal values need validation in a contrast tool; Golos versus IBM Plex Sans should be tested on real dense tables; the task-system decision determines whether the Work modules exist at all; and the AI-first entry needs a usability check with real analysts, comparing ask-first and browse-first for finding approved documents.
